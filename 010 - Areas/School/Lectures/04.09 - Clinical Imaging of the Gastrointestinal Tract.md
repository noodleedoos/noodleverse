# Intended Learning Outcomes

• Identify abdominal organs on radiographs

• Determine if serosal detail is reduced

• Determine if abdominal fluid is in the peritoneum or retroperitoneum

• Determine if a line indicating the gastric axis is correctly placed

• Determine the size of the liver using the gastric axis

• Identify the head of the spleen

• Recall that the head of the spleen is seen on the VD view & the tail of the spleen is seen on the lateral view

• Determine if the tail of the spleen can be seen on a lateral view in a cat and recall that this indicates splenomegaly

• Evaluate kidney size by comparison to L2 (normal reference ranges do not need to be remembered - you can look this up in practice)

• Recall that this is only done on the VD view

• Evaluate the appearance of the kidneys, recalling that oSen only parts of the kidneys are seen in dogs

• Determine if the content of the GIT is normal on radiographs

• Determine the location of the fundus and pylorus

• Determine if the width of the small intestine is normal. Recall the normal raDo with the vertebra in cats and dogs.

• Determine if some segments of small intestine are abnormally large compared to others

• Identify the location of the duodenum & caecum. Recall the expected location on a radiograph even if you cannot see it. Recall that the caecum is not seen in cats.

• Determine the normal location of the colon, including at the pelvic inlet.

• Recall the location of the organs in the caudal abdomen on the lateral projection. Label those that can be seen or the expected locaDon of those that are unseen.

• Recall the 4 locations for abdominal masses

• Recall the diﬀerences between cats and dogs
