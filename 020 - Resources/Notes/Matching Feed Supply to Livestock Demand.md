# PGR and Pasture Height - Grazing Calculations

## How long can these sheep stay in the paddock?
- Stock introduced to paddock when it is 2500 kgDM/ha and removed when 1500 kgDM/ha.
- If paddock 10 ha and pasture growing 50 kgDM/ha/d
- Assume each sheep eats 1 kgDM/day (depends on grass quality)
- 750 wethers in paddock, how long can they stay?
~
- Know there is 1000 available kg/DM at start (2500-1500), 10 ha = 10,000 kg/DM.
- Each day weathers eat 750kg but paddock grows 10x50 (500), so each day amount is reduced by 250 kg
Wethers can stay in paddock for 40 days 
## How long until we graze the paddock again?
- start 1500kgDM/ha, get back to 2500kgDM/ha
- We can divide this total by growth per day to calculate number of days
- If growing at 50kgDM/ha/d then would be 20 days before next rotational graze (1000/50 = 20 days), but
- If growing at 20kgDM/ha/d then would be 50 days before next rotational graze (1000/20 = 50 days)
- This would be like comparing winter and spring PGR
- To manage during spring it is common for producers to not graze some paddocks and cut for silage/hay

## Sell Livestock Considerations
*Describe what animals you should sell on a self replacing enterprise vs non-breeding enterprise*
~
Depends on replacing or non-replacing
**Self replacing Beef cattle enterprise**
1. Initial sale animals (stock for fattening)
2. older pregnant cattle (5+years)
3. Young female weaner (up to 2 y.)
4. Keep 2-5y. as breeding stock
**Non Breeding Enterprise**
- Sell animals with lowest growth rates

## Agist/Lease Property
*what is the diffference, what are the positives and negatives?*
~
**Agistment** - payment per animal over a short period
**Leasing** - contract to manage farm for a specific period of years
During drought ->agist/lease to areas not affected or where there is water irrigation
**Benefits**: Less supplementary feed
**Negatives**: locations normally far from original place, hard to know stock safety, biosecurity, who monitors and how long for
