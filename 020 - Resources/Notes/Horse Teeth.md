# Dentition
*When do horse teeth grow and erupt?*
~
- Grow from apical part until 17 years
- Erupt for life
<!--SR:!2025-04-07,14,290-->

## Incisors
*Describe the appearance of horse incisors*
~
- Dentine exposed at occlusal surface
- Infundibulum/Cup/Mark disappears with age
- Pulp cavity disappears with age, protected by dentine
- Star made of tertiary dentine which is stained if eating plant material. 
- Star appears above cup because star is up above :3

## Canines
*Describe the difference in canines in males vs females (amount and eruption time). What kind of teeth are they and what is their function and structure?*
~
Stallions and Geldings mostly have 4 that erupt from 4-6 years old
Mares - canines absent/rudimentary
Found in diastema
Brachydont because they don't grow continually, but they have a long reserve crown
Unknown function, maybe fighting

## Premolars/Molars
*Describe the cheek teeth in horses, when does the special tooth erupt? What are these teeth called together? What covers the crown? What might happen to the teeth and why?*
~
P1 = wolf teeth, erupts at 6-12 months old (baby wolf!)
AKA table
Enamel not continuous, some dentine exposed, cementum around enamel crown
Maxillary teeth wider apart and wider than mandibular, result in sharp points on buccal aspect of maxillary and lingual aspect of mandibular called hooks that require floating (filing)

# Teeth Formation
*when do roots form in horse teeth?*
~
Roots only form in old horse

*Where might you expect to find canine and wolf teeth?*
~
Diastema
Wolf teeth not in all horses, more frequently in lower arcade, sometimes not on both sides. First premolar tooth with no deciduous

# Dentine
*Dentine role and sensitivity in horse teeth, and why*
~
Primary dentine - during tooth development under enamel
Secondary dentine prevents pulp exposure
Tertiary dentine sometimes laid down for the same thing
At occlusal surface not sensitive due to calcification

# Teeth Function
*Cheek Teeth*
~
Continous flat grinding surface with convoluted enamel ridges

*Sharp edges where*
~
Buccal side of upper cheek teeth
Lingual side of lower cheek teeth

# Roots
*Why are tooth roots so long?*
~
Extensive reserve crown llows eruption to replace crown as it is worn down
Also allow firm holding during mastication

# Impacts on Dental wear
~
Diet: tough plants or high energy grains
Stabling: sterotypical behaviour like crib biting

# Importance of regular dental examination
~
Reluctance to eat due to sharp edges can lead to injury

# Molar tooth Removal
~
When? old horses affected by periodontal disease
Upper teeth: cut hole in maxilla, push it out through oral cavity
Lower teeth: cut hole in alveolus, lift tooth out
