# Definition
~
Close link, but distinct membranes 
Small synaptic cleft 
Neurotransmitters bridge cleft 
Most synapses are this type

# Presynaptic Nerve Terminal and Synaptic Cleft
- Presynaptic nerve terminal - Axon enlargement 
	- Release of signalling molecules 
- Synaptic cleft - Narrow gap 
	- Neurotransmitters diffuse across 
	- Postsynaptic membrane/element Dendrite, cell body, target cell

# Chemical Synaptic Transmission
~
1. Action potential arrives at presynaptic terminal 
2. Voltage gated Ca2+ channels are opened 
3. Ca2+ signals to neurotransmitter vesicles to release (released in proportion to Ca2+ influx) 
4. Vesicles dock/fuse with membrane (exocytosis) 
5. Release contents across synaptic cleft 
6. Bind with receptors on post-synaptic membrane 
7. Post synaptic effects 
8. Clean up of neurotransmitter in synaptic cleft 
	1. Pinocytosis (reuptake to presynaptic) Enzyme degradation

# Post Synaptic Response
~
Depends on the type of receptor activated In many cases will activate an ion channel 
- Depolarising – excitatory - [[EPSP]] e.g. Ca2+ and Na+
- Hyperpolarising – inhibitory - [[IPSP]] e.g. Cl- and negative ions
EPSP and IPSP will last a few milliseconds (Na/K ATPase and K+ leak channels restore) 
Multiple axons can synapse on a cell 
- Effect is the summation of EPSP and IPSP
- Net effect of EPSP and IPSP summates at axon hillock 
- If is sufficient stimulus, will lead to AP
