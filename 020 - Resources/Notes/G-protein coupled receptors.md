# Description
*Location, time scale, mechanism and effect*
~
Integral in cell membrane
Fast (seconds) and common for drugs
# Function
~
1. Hormone binding induces conformational change to GPCR (activates)
2. Receptor binds to Ga subunit
3. The GDP bound to the Ga dissociates and is replaced by GTP, Ga dissociates
4. Hormone dissociates from receptor while Ga binds to effector (activates)
5. Hydrolysis of GTP to GDP causes Ga to dissociate from effector and reassociate
Result: ion influx, secondary messenger release ([[cAMP]], [[cGMP]], [[DAG]], [[IP3]], [[Ca2+]]) which can stimulate or inhibit effector molecules like [[Phospholipase C]], [[Adenylyl cyclase]] and [[calcium channels]]

# Structure and Components
~
Largest of cell surface receptors
7 Transmembrane domains, loop inside cell between 5-6 couples with the the intracellular effector system via G-proteins
Heterotrimeric G-Protein (3 subunits bound together)
- There are at least 23 α subunits, 12γ subunits, and 6 β subunits

# 4 Regulatory Points
~
1. Ligand-Receptor Interaction
Ligand induced receptors are internalised in clathrin coated pits. Once internalised, Beta arrestins bind to GPCR to downregulate the response, desensitising it.
2. "Ground state" Gαβγ protein reforms resulting in the deactivation of adenylate cyclase when GTP hydrolyses to GDP
![](https://i.imgur.com/LDeiqZY.png)
3. Degradation of cAMP->AMP by a [[Phosphodiesterase]]
4. Intracellular Protein Phosphatases deactivate enzymes like PKA by removing phosphate groups.

## Hormones Acting Additively
~
[[Glucagon]] and [[adrenalin]] act via Gs proteins on liver to increase AC->cAMP
Result: Increased glucagon breakdown and release of glucose to bloodstream
