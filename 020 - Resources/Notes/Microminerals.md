# Description
*Quantity and role*
~
- Present in body tissue at concentrations <50 mg/kg (50 ppm)
- Tend to be cofactors for enzymes
	- zinc important in enzymes in turnover with connective tissues

# Examples
Chromium, manganese, iron, cobalt, molybdenum, copper, zinc, fluoride, iodine, selenium, silicon, tin, arsenic, nickel.

# Diseases Trends
*Give 4 examples of diseases with low trace elements and why the trends are like that*
~
Low cobalt in sandy areas (gippsland coast)
Low copper - sandy areas plus higher country
Low Selenium - more widespread, but also mountainous areas and sandy areas in late winter early spring
Low Iodine - [[Goitre]] in high rainfall areas like high country
Trends by certain areas, soil types/climate
Result: low available levels of the relevant trace elements

# Importance and Considerations
*Why is it important to monitor trace elements and what might cause issues? Give an example*
~
Not always noticeable at subclinical levels
Can overdose due to supplement, vaccines/drench - toxicity e.g. stock grazing Selenium accumulating plants

# Trace Element deficiency
*When is it common and how can we diagnose it?*
~
Winter/spring in high rainfall
Enlarged thyroid, some need blood testt or production trials
Subclinical signs
Decrease immunity/enzyme function
Decrease maximum performance/reproduction
