# Definition
*What is it? What is the associated value? and what does that mean?*
~
The electrical potential difference across the cell membrane when the cell is in a resting state
- Cell membrane forms ‘semi/selectively permeable’ barrier Proteins/channels/transporters allow the cell to control its activity 
- Typically -70mV (intracellular is 70mV less than the extracellular)

# Ion concentrations
*Describe the concentration of 3 ions extracellularly and intracellularly, and how it is maintained in 2 ways, and the cell's energy use*
- Extracellular, intracellular
- Na+ and Cl- big, small
- K+ small, big
- Also HCO3- extracellular; proteins and PO4 intracellular
**Maintained by**
- [[Na+K+ ATPase pump]] responsible for 75% of cell's energy use
- K+ leak channels

# Clinical Points
*Describe how changes in ion concentrations impacts the cell and other molecules (What impacts cell excitability? How can hyperkalaemia be managed? What is the role of Na+? What happens to osmolarity when ions move?*
~
1. Changes in ECF K+ can have dramatic effects on cell excitability, There is a narrow acceptable range. 
2. There is a huge buffer for K+ intracellularly (helpful for managing hyperkalaemia). 
3. Na+ balance is key to RMP and cell excitability 
4. Na+ and Cl- tend to travel together 
5. When ions move, they affect osmolarity, so H20 tries to move too
